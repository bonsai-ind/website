The Why
We track almost everything that matters to us. Deep space. Engines. Markets. But the most intense biological stretch of a human life plays out in the dark, and nobody writes it down.

In those first 1,000 days a baby's brain builds around a million new connections every second. Its body sets the baselines it will keep for life: how it breathes, how it holds temperature, how it fights off infection. And the way we watch over all of that today is a tired parent standing in a dark room, hoping they're reading it right.

Two and a half years ago, one of us was that parent. 3 AM, standing over a quiet crib, trying to guess if something was slowly going wrong. The thing is, it almost always leaves a trace first. A pause in breathing is a change in rhythm. A fever is a change in heat. A cry carries a pattern. The signs are usually there before the emergency is. We're just the first ones bothering to write them down.

The Brutal Reality

The invisible trail.

A change in temperature. A pause in breathing. A cry that keeps climbing. Most monitors just watch and beep. They never tell you what any of it means.

27%

What is Vera.1?+

Vera.1 is an AI infant monitor made by Inspirium Tech, a startup based in Ahmedabad, India. It watches your baby's breathing, temperature, heart rate and cries from across the room, with nothing attached to the baby, and tells you what's actually going on instead of just sounding an alarm.

How does it monitor a baby without any wearables?+

It uses a camera, an infrared thermal sensor and a microphone together. Breathing shows up as movement, fever shows up as heat, and cries carry patterns. The AI reads all three at once, so nothing needs to touch your baby's skin. No socks, no straps, no wires.

Can it really tell why a baby is crying?+

It tells cries apart by pitch, length and pattern, then combines that with what the other sensors see. So instead of "your baby is crying," you get "probably hungry, it's been about three hours since the last feed." When a cry looks like real distress, it treats it as an emergency and calls you.

What health problems can it catch early?+

A fever building before you'd feel it by hand. Changes in breathing rhythm. Dangerous situations, like a blanket over the face. Signs like these usually show up before an emergency does, and catching them early is the whole point of the product.

How is Vera.1 different from Nanit, Owlet or Cubo AI?+

Nanit and Cubo are smart cameras; they can't read vitals. Owlet and Masimo's Stork read vitals but need something strapped to the baby. Vera.1 does both jobs at once, vitals plus cry understanding, with nothing worn by the baby.

Is it meant for hospitals too?+

Yes. We're starting with homes, and working with NICUs and pediatric hospitals to validate it clinically. Today's NICU sensors stick to a newborn's skin and can damage it. Vera.1 reads the same signals from a distance.
